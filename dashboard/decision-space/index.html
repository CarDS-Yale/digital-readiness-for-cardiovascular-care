About this chart

This is the interactive version of the manuscript's burden-readiness decision space. Each dot is one census tract in the workforce-constrained county pool, placed by digital readiness (Digital Divide Index, x axis) and cardiometabolic burden (composite z score, y axis).

The classification

The pool holds counties with no cardiologist in 2023 or a declining number of cardiologists per 100,000 residents over 2010 to 2023. Within it, two anchored cuts form a two-by-two matrix:

high burden      : burden z > 0 (national mean)
higher readiness : DDI ≤ 18.8 (national median)

DEPLOYMENT PRIORITY = high burden AND higher readiness
INVESTMENT PRIORITY = high burden AND lower readiness

Ranks come from a priority score: burden and DDI are standardized within the pool, then combined as burden minus DDI for deployment priority and burden plus DDI for investment priority. These labels indicate relative priorities under the chosen thresholds. They do not establish readiness for any specific intervention.

The burden composite

Ten conditions and risk factors from CDC PLACES (hypertension, high cholesterol, coronary heart disease, stroke, diabetes, obesity, smoking, physical inactivity, short sleep, binge drinking), averaged across the 2022 and 2023 releases, standardized across tracts, and averaged into one z score.

Kentucky and Pennsylvania

CDC suppressed nine of the ten measures at the tract level in these two states, so their burden score rests on short sleep alone. Their tracts keep group membership and count toward the national totals, but they are not plotted here and are excluded from the ranked lists.

Using the chart

Hover any dot for a mini profile; click it for the full tract panel. The legend chips show or hide each group. The Highlight control spotlights the 10, 50, or 100 leading tracts in each group. View as table lists the same leading tracts as a ranked, keyboard-friendly table.

Robustness

The manuscript tests this framework in eight sensitivity analyses, spanning alternative readiness measures, workforce definitions, thresholds, and a geographic access credit. Classifications were broadly stable; a six-indicator readiness index placed tracts in the same group about 93% of the time.

Data sources and accessibility

HRSA Area Health Resources Files (2010-2023), CDC PLACES (2022-2023 releases), and the Purdue Digital Divide Index (2022-2024, built from ACS and FCC data). Both dashboards were designed to meet WCAG 2.1 Level AA. The companion county atlas maps every view at county and tract level.
