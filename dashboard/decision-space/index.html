About this chart

This is the interactive version of the manuscript's burden-readiness decision space. Each dot is one census tract in the workforce-constrained county pool, placed by digital readiness (Digital Divide Index, x axis) and cardiometabolic burden (composite z score, y axis).

The classification

The pool holds counties with no cardiologist in 2023 or a declining number of cardiologists per 100,000 residents over 2010 to 2023. Within it, two anchored cuts form a two-by-two matrix:

high burden      : burden z > 0 (national mean)
higher readiness : DDI ≤ 18.8 (national median)

DEPLOYMENT PRIORITY = high burden AND higher readiness
INVESTMENT PRIORITY = high burden AND lower readiness

Ranks come from a priority score: burden and DDI are standardized within the pool, then combined as burden minus DDI for deployment priority and burden plus DDI for investment priority. These labels indicate relative priorities under the chosen thresholds. They do not establish readiness for any specific intervention.

Setting your own thresholds

The Thresholds control moves the cuts. The presets are the national percentiles the manuscript's threshold analysis uses: burden above the mean, in the top quartile or in the top decile, crossed with a median, quartile or decile split of the DDI. A quartile or decile split leaves a middle band of tracts in neither group. The priority scores are fixed, so a stricter cut keeps the study ordering and drops the tracts that miss it, while a looser cut admits tracts in their score order. The counts in the header follow your settings and include Kentucky and Pennsylvania.

Restricting the view

Search a state, county or metropolitan area to restrict the view. Tracts outside the area fade, the counts switch to the area, and the Leading tracts list ranks the area's tracts under the current cuts. Metropolitan areas follow the 2023 core-based statistical area definitions carried in the Area Health Resources Files.

The burden composite

Ten conditions and risk factors from CDC PLACES (hypertension, high cholesterol, coronary heart disease, stroke, diabetes, obesity, smoking, physical inactivity, short sleep, binge drinking), from the 2025 census tract release, standardized across tracts and averaged into one z score.

Kentucky and Pennsylvania

CDC suppressed nine of the ten measures at the tract level in these two states, so their burden score rests on short sleep alone. Their tracts keep group membership and count toward the totals, but they are not plotted here and are excluded from the ranked lists.

Using the chart

Hover any dot for a mini profile; click it for the full tract panel. The legend chips show or hide each group. The Leading tracts list shows the 10, 25, 50 or 100 highest-ranked tracts in each group under the current thresholds and scope, spotlights them on the chart, and downloads as a CSV.

Robustness

The manuscript tests this framework in nine sensitivity analyses, spanning alternative readiness measures, workforce definitions, thresholds, and a geographic access credit, and checks each input against an independent source. Classifications were broadly stable; a six-indicator readiness index placed tracts in the same group about 93% of the time.

Data sources and accessibility

HRSA Area Health Resources Files (2010-2023), CDC PLACES (2025 release), and the Purdue Digital Divide Index (2022-2024, built from ACS and FCC data). Both dashboards were designed to meet WCAG 2.1 Level AA. The companion county atlas maps every view at county and tract level.
